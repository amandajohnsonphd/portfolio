Revenue Impact Mixed Methods Product Growth

Using Mixed-Methods Research to Double Revenue Coverage for a Browser Feature

Opt-in adoption for Link History plateaued below targets, especially among older users. A cross-functional v-team combining log analysis and qualitative interviews uncovered the real barriers — not feature value, but unclear language and poor onboarding timing.

Grew revenue coverage from 21% to 42%, surpassing adoption targets across multiple platforms

Link History user flow: opt-in prompt → settings navigation → recently visited links view

The Problem

Link History was a major strategic investment enabling high-value first-party behavioral signals tied to monetization. Following launch, adoption plateaued well below targets, particularly among users 45+.

Approach

  • Stood up a v-team of UXR, Product Growth, and Data Scientists
  • Log analysis to identify segments with lower opt-in rates and drop-off points
  • Qualitative interviews to surface motivational barriers log data couldn't reveal

Key Findings

  • Users 45+ opted in at significantly lower rates despite being top requesters of the feature
  • Onboarding language around "ads personalization" was misinterpreted as receiving more ads
  • Opt-in appeared at disruptive, contextually irrelevant moments
  • Barriers were driven by unclear value exchange, confusing language, and poor timing — not lack of feature interest

Impact

  • Grew revenue coverage from 21% to 42%
  • Influenced launch of a high-performing experiment variant with statistically significant opt-in increases shipped to production
  • Reshaped the experimentation roadmap toward timing, contextual relevance, and clearer value communication — later influencing cross-functional experimentation across additional teams
Product Strategy Quantitative Growth

Informing Product Strategy That Contributed to Double-Digit User Growth at Massive Scale

A browser platform serving hundreds of millions of users needed to identify the highest-impact product investments. MaxDiff, Conjoint, and large-scale on-platform surveys built the quantitative foundation for a three-year product roadmap.

~15% user growth over 3 years; shaped a multi-year product strategy

The Problem

Growing pressure to prioritize investments most likely to drive long-term engagement and growth across a massive browser ecosystem with hundreds of millions of users.

Approach

  • MaxDiff and Conjoint analysis to identify which features users valued most and which bundles would drive adoption
  • Large-scale on-platform surveys to quantify delight and frustration drivers across the browser ecosystem

Key Findings

  • Users prioritized perceived security and easy content revisit; price comparison ranked highly
  • Browser performance, accidental clicks, and low perceived security drove disengagement
  • Trust and usability were deeply interconnected — performance shaped perceptions of safety

Impact

  • Shaped and prioritized a three-year product strategy grounded in quantitative user evidence
  • Strategy contributed to approximately 15% user growth over three years
  • Directed future investments toward browser performance, accidental click reduction, and perceived security
Agentic AI Concept Testing Launch Strategy

Driving Product Strategy for Next-Generation Agentic Browsing

For a first-of-its-kind agentic browser, research revealed that value-centered framing dramatically outperformed AI-first messaging — and that user control needed to be front and center in onboarding.

Shifted launch strategy from AI-centered to value-centered positioning; inspired new onboarding concepts

The Problem

The team was preparing to launch a first-of-its-kind agentic in-app browser. User trust, comprehension, and perceived value would be critical to adoption.

Approach

  • Content-focused concept survey evaluating how different messaging, framing, and value propositions influenced user interest, trust, and likelihood to adopt

Key Findings

  • Value-centered framing significantly outperformed AI-forward framing at p < .001
  • Unnecessary "with AI" labeling triggered privacy concerns when not paired with data explanations
  • Users prioritized ability to manage settings and controls at any time over personalized promotions
  • AI iconography was recognized but context was unclear; lightweight tooltips improved comprehension

Impact

  • Shifted launch strategy from AI-centered to value-centered positioning focused on user benefit and control
  • Inspired new product and onboarding concepts that reframed data controls as a core user value proposition
Revenue Wins Data Analysis Experimentation

Driving Research-Informed Experimentation Strategy That Delivered Multiple Revenue Wins

As Autofill transitioned to a new product team, Python-based log analysis of contact data quality surfaced low-lift, high-impact reliability improvements — leading to six experimentation wins and measurable revenue gains within six months.

6 experimentation wins contributing measurable revenue gains within 6 months

The Problem

Autofill was transitioning ownership in H1 2026. The team needed a browser-specific evidence base to identify adoption barriers, improve trust, and shape a product strategy capable of driving meaningful revenue impact.

Approach

  • Internal and competitive literature + research synthesis to surface known barriers and unmet needs
  • Large-scale log analysis of Autofill contact data quality using Python to investigate reliability issues

Key Findings

  • Inconsistent Autofill triggering significantly reduced user confidence and willingness to enter information
  • State/ZIP mismatches, duplicate entries, and invalid phone formatting were low-lift improvement opportunities
  • Security and trust messaging emerged as a critical unmet need

Impact

  • Shaped a research-driven product strategy contributing to measurable revenue gains across six experimentation wins in six months
  • Improved Autofill UX through increased reliability and higher-quality saved data
  • Informed future design and content research on security communication
Regulatory Risk Accessibility Cross-Functional

Using Accessibility Research to De-Risk Billions in Potential Revenue Exposure

Proactively identified that the European Accessibility Act would impact a browser serving billions of users. Led a cross-functional initiative that helped the team achieve compliance readiness ahead of enforcement deadlines and secured leadership funding for an ongoing accessibility program.

Positioned browser team as an early compliance leader; secured leadership sponsorship for a dedicated accessibility initiative

The Problem

The EAA, taking effect June 2025, introduced compliance requirements with penalties up to 5% of annual global revenue. The mobile browser served billions of users but had never undergone a formal accessibility review, and accessibility had not been identified as a strategic priority.

Approach

  • Minimum viable accessibility review of core browser flows with assistive technology users
  • Iterative usability testing on in-development features with assistive technology users
  • Assembled and led a cross-functional v-team including design, content design, engineering, and program management

Key Findings

  • Core browser flows failed to meet minimum accessibility viability standards
  • A newly proposed footer design was inaccessible to screen reader users, blocking high-value promotional content
  • Many accessibility gaps also represented broader usability issues

Impact

  • Helped position the browser team as one of the earliest groups to achieve compliance readiness ahead of EAA enforcement deadlines
  • Secured leadership sponsorship and funding for a dedicated accessibility initiative
  • Recommended accessible footer pattern adopted as design standard for future development
  • Established an accessibility testing framework for ongoing product development
Ad Revenue Qualitative Experimentation

How Qualitative Research Informed High-Impact Ad Revenue Experiments

A ML team needed to prioritize which product metadata signals to test on pre-click ads. Qualitative interviews revealed that availability and social proof — not urgency signals — drove user click decisions, enabling the team to focus experimentation on highest-confidence opportunities first.

Multiple metadata signals achieved statistically significant ad performance improvements; collectively exceeded revenue targets for the half

Example of product metadata labeling (e.g. “IN STOCK”) surfaced on pre-click ad unit

The Problem

Dozens of potential metadata signals to test, each requiring meaningful investment. The team needed to understand which signals users actually valued before deciding what to test first.

Approach

  • Qualitative interviews focused on how users evaluate ads and what information influences pre-click decisions
  • Method chosen because the core question was about how users make decisions, not which label they prefer

Key Findings

  • Users' core pre-click question was: "Is what I'm looking for actually available?"
  • Availability and attribute metadata (colors, sizes in stock) generated the strongest positive response
  • Social proof signals ("Bestseller") functioned as trust shortcuts
  • Urgency signals were perceived skeptically, associated with pressure tactics rather than helpful information

Impact

  • Findings used to prioritize which metadata signals to test first
  • Informed label language, positioning, and content hierarchy within experiments
  • Multiple validated signals achieved statistically significant improvements; collectively exceeded revenue targets for the half
Mental Health Participatory Design Mobile App

Designing a Mental Health Crisis Intervention App Through Participatory Research

Conducted participatory design and concept testing with individuals who had lived experience of mental health crises to co-create a mobile intervention app for high-risk moments. Developed an ethical research framework for this vulnerable population.

App rated highly on usability and perceived usefulness; research methodology presented at multiple conferences

Connections app: Safety Plan access, coping tool navigation, and supportive messaging — designed through participatory research with lived-experience participants

The Problem

Individuals in crisis are often unable or unwilling to access support at the moment they need it. The goal was to design a mobile experience providing accessible support during high-risk moments.

Approach

  • Participatory design sessions with individuals who had experienced mental health crises and suicidality
  • Concept testing to evaluate whether app concepts felt supportive, trustworthy, and usable during acute distress

Key Findings

  • A major crisis driver was perceived disconnection from resources for immediate life stressors (job loss, housing, relationships)
  • Individuals had difficulty recalling coping strategies during acute distress
  • Reminders of coping tools and support systems were perceived as potentially grounding and protective
  • Trust, simplicity, and low cognitive burden were critical design requirements

Impact

  • Designed and tested a mobile crisis support app rated highly on usability and perceived usefulness
  • Developed a replicable ethical research framework for participatory research with high-risk mental health populations
  • Presented design and methodology at multiple conferences
Healthcare Human Factors Behavioral Coding

Using Human Factors Research to Optimize Robot-Assisted Surgery Team Performance

Analyzed synchronized video recordings of 12 robot-assisted surgeries to understand how team anticipation and familiarity influenced OR efficiency, communication, and surgeon cognitive load. Higher anticipation rates were associated with an 8% reduction in OR time.

8% reduction in OR time associated with higher team anticipation; findings informed surgical training program development

The Problem

Robot-assisted surgery changes traditional OR workflows, physically separating console surgeons from patients and team members — disrupting communication, coordination, and situational awareness.

Approach

  • Observation and behavioral coding of synchronized video/audio from 12 robot-assisted radical prostatectomies
  • Coded 1,330 requests by personnel, equipment type, communication modality, inconvenience level, and whether anticipated
  • Quantitative measures of team familiarity, cognitive workload (NASA-TLX), request duration, and communication patterns

Key Findings

  • 31% of requests were anticipated before verbalization
  • Higher anticipation rates were associated with shorter operative times — an 8% reduction in OR time
  • Greater team familiarity correlated with fewer workflow inconveniences
  • Anticipation ratio, non-verbal requests, and request duration were significantly associated with surgeon cognitive load

Impact

  • Informed development of training programs focused on anticipation behaviors and team familiarity
  • Contributed to broader human factors understanding of coordination in technologically complex surgical environments
